What do the two dollar amounts mean?

Total USD activity counts all eligible transactions involving selected accounts. Laundering-labeled USD activity counts only those transactions whose synthetic source label is positive. Both count each transaction once and use the same September 1–10 history.

Why does total activity jump at rank 12?

One synthetic account enters at rank 12 with $52.76B of eligible historical USD activity, but its 243 laundering-labeled transactions total $50.05M. The labeled amount now uses the individual transaction flag, not the account’s whole history. Dollar amounts describe synthetic benchmark activity, not confirmed crime, recovered funds, or losses prevented.

The ranking selects account-days. The dollar cards show account histories at the September 10 end-of-day review snapshot. A selected account’s earlier labeled transactions qualify even when its selected account-day has a negative label. Labels are used only for retrospective evaluation, not to choose the review queue.

One ranking. Two different units of analysis.

The model ranks account-days. Transaction volume is calculated from the distinct accounts represented in that ranking, using their available history through the fixed review snapshot. It describes the scale of activity available for review.

What counts toward each amount?

Total USD activity includes every eligible source transaction involving at least one selected account. Laundering-labeled USD activity includes only those same transactions whose raw Is Laundering flag equals 1. Each transaction enters each metric only once, even if both endpoints or multiple days are selected. Positive labels do not determine which accounts enter the queue.

History, currencies, and double counting

History runs September 1–10, 2022, inclusive, as observed at the September 10 end-of-day snapshot. Only transactions with both payment and receiving currencies equal to US Dollar are included; the amount paid is used. Each original source row is treated as one transaction, including genuine repeated rows. A transfer involving two selected accounts is counted once. These histories must not be interpreted as information already known at an earlier alert date.

Denominators and customer identity

Total-activity coverage divides by all eligible unique USD activity linked to any account in the 494,707-account-day test population. The labeled share divides laundering-labeled dollars in the selected queue by total USD activity in that same queue. It answers how much of the review amount carries the synthetic laundering label. The downloadable export also includes labeled-dollar coverage against the full eligible population, as a separate measure. Accounts are not assumed to be customers: no customer-to-account mapping is available. Review counts and F1 remain at account-day level.

Source and limitations

IBM synthetic HI-Small transactions and the canonical 389-positive ranking at the original 2,747 cutoff. Raw account-day labels were reconciled against all 494,707 ranking rows. All 494,707 cutoffs were checked for maximum F1; the interactive view displays exact results through 15,000. No model was retrained. Amounts are synthetic transaction turnover, not customer balances, recovered funds, or measured review effort. Positive labels are available for retrospective evaluation and would not be known when initially selecting a real queue.
